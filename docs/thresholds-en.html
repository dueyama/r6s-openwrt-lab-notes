The result: beta near one is a reasonable baseline within the saved scope

Beta is a sensitivity coefficient for adjusting the decision threshold while checking an acceptable false-positive rate. At 0.5, saved C72 history gives a per-score false-positive-equivalent range of about 0.0000198–0.0111%, conditional on all eligible traffic being benign. Even its upper bound is below the paper’s 0.1% evaluation level. Within this saved scope, the evidence does not require increasing beta further to meet that reference.

This is neither an established FPR nor a future guarantee. The 0.1% level is a comparison reference from the paper, not a newly selected fixed user target. These aggregates cannot establish a rate at or below 0.01%. The small rounding difference between the chosen operational value 0.5 and exact beta=1 is distinguished below.

What beta adjusts

T = φ × β

T is the decision threshold, phi the maximum output-layer RMSE recorded during training, and beta a multiplier of that baseline. RMSE measures reconstruction error and serves as an anomaly score here; it is not an intrusion probability.

For the same fixed scores, increasing beta raises T and cannot increase exceedances, but may also miss anomalies. Computing beta alone does not pass an FPR target. Define the population and denominator, count exceedances on known-benign data, and assess the false-positive/missed-detection trade-off.

The paper’s §III-E uses beta≥1 and records training maxima from pre-update errors; §IV-A retains the output-layer training maximum. §IV-D treats the maximum rule as a simple approach and also discusses distribution-based selection. Beta=1 is not a universal network optimum.

Primary source: Kitsune §§III-E, IV-A, IV-D

The paper also uses beta for a compression ratio—0.75 in this experiment’s settings—which is a different parameter. Its §III-E procedure uses s≥T, whereas this saved reassessment uses s>T. Boundary semantics were not silently interchanged.

The origin of 0.22 was not the training maximum

On October 26, 2026, 0.22 was proposed slightly above the maximum CPU64 C72 score, 0.21465562979249048, on 5,120 out-of-training inputs. Three-hour observations then examined how often scores exceeded it. C72 and D168 are different models trained for 72 hours/three days and 168 hours/seven days respectively.

This was a reference for starting observation. Later evaluation under an ordinary-household-use assumption is separate from its original selection. A training-time maximum phi, a final model’s maximum on selected re-scored inputs, and a live one-minute maximum are different statistics.

What minute maxima and counts can establish about per-score FPR

FPR = FP / (FP + TN)

FP counts benign scores flagged positive; TN counts benign scores not flagged. Assuming every detection was false does not alone establish FP+TN: unflagged items might be benign or missed anomalies. The following adds the assumption that all eligible valid scores were benign. This is not independently verified benign ground truth.

1 / 5,053,722 ≤ FPR* ≤ 560 / 5,053,722

As percentages, the range is about 0.0000197874–0.01108094%. A maximum above 0.5 establishes at least one exceedance; at most all 560 scores in that bin exceeded it. This is an identified range from maxima/counts, not a point estimate, statistical confidence interval or established FPR.

Within the 0.1% reference; 0.01% is not established

The paper’s §§V-C/D evaluate performance at FPR=0 and 0.001; the latter fraction is 0.1%. The conditional upper bound here, 0.01108094%, is below 0.1%, supporting beta near one as a baseline for this C saved-data scope against that reference. The beta setting does not automatically guarantee FPR. Equal C/D phi does not transfer this C72 singleton-history rate to D168 operation: D’s 0.5 is the user’s choice, and D outcomes require separate observation.

Primary source: FPR definition and evaluation levels, §§V-C/D

The upper bound exceeds 0.01%, so a rate at or below 0.01% is not established; the exact individual exceedance count is unknown. Our unit is a representative NPU score from a nonempty 100-ms interval, not the paper’s per-packet unit. These results do not establish superiority to the paper, future rates or adequate attack recall.

A later operational review needs a stated acceptable FPR/unit, validation on a separate known-benign period, and separate missed-detection measurement. Retrospectively selecting a cutoff on the same history is not independent future validation. These are limits of this judgment, not an automatically scheduled experiment.

November 1, 2026: 0.5 applied and checked through normal cycles and readers

Following training-maximum reconstruction and saved-C72 reassessment, the user-approved D168 operational rule changed to strict >0.5 at 11:39 JST on November 1. Saved acceptance records confirm the same policy on R6S and Pi 5, acceptance by the existing reader with its original 17 checks, and decision_threshold=0.5 in the separate UI API. This was not model retraining.

Only the three existing Pi 5 notification/context/analysis timers paused for about 97 seconds, then resumed their original schedules. All three normal-cycle services subsequently exited successfully, while NPU scores advanced with unchanged execution identity. NPU operation and normal traffic continued; old notification identifiers, cooldown state, history and existing jobs were retained. No extra paid AI call or test notification was triggered for acceptance.

Three-hour observation uses a separate comparison policy, also strict >0.5. The old 0.22 code/state is retained, with a new observation-only accumulator beginning at 10:31 JST on November 1, separately from the 11:39 operational start. The first read succeeded: zero exceeding bins among six finalized post-baseline minutes, and zero among 659 retained day-window minutes. The latter is a retrospective 0.5 comparison over about 11 retained hours, not a complete 24-hour observation. Missing-score counts remain unmeasured; zero exceedances do not establish detection performance or an FPR.

This acceptance establishes policy adoption and consistency through normal cycles, readers and the API. It does not establish a newly tested post-cutover natural exceedance flowing through notification, context and Terra analysis. A later same-day r62 acceptance completed desktop/narrow UI checks including the wording. That version-specific visual acceptance does not automatically cover the review UI after later NPU configuration changes. Historical notification text was not rewritten to 0.5.
